# Writing an exam

Issue #54: a paper kyto wrote copied two of Coolton's questions, kept topics from the draft it said it threw away, used private channel material, and shipped a "rubric" instead of an answer key. Every step below closes one of those.

1. **Constraint list first.** Before researching, write down (in your own working, not the thread) every rule the asker gave: banned topics, the paper being replaced, "no private channels", length, format, time limit. Re-read the whole thread for rules given later than the ask.
2. **What's already taken.** Collect every question in context that isn't yours to reuse: the original exam, other bots' papers in the thread, and your own rejected draft. When you're told to replace a draft, its TOPICS are gone too, not just its wording, unless the asker says otherwise.
3. **Research public sources only.** Search wide in one batch (`searchSlack`, `searchWeb`), then read the hits in full. A question whose answer only lives in a private channel, a DM or this thread can't go in: the people taking the test can't check it.
4. **One fact, one source.** Each question gets an answer and a permalink or URL proving it. If you can't find a source, drop the question. Don't soften it into "any reasonable answer" or invent a rubric. A long-answer question still names the specific facts a full answer must contain, each with its source.
5. **Make it VERY DETAILED.** A short or vague paper is a failed paper. Every question spells out exactly what to do, the inputs or data it works from, the expected answer format, and what counts as a complete answer, so two graders would score the same answer the same way. Prefer multi-part questions that need real work over one-liners.
6. **Say how it is graded, in the paper itself.** Points per question and per part, and the total. For each, what earns full marks, what earns partial marks, and what earns zero, plus any named deductions (a missing source, a wrong unit, an unsupported claim). The answer key carries the same scheme with the expected answer under each item.
7. **Say how time affects the grade.** Give the time limit, when the clock starts, and what timestamp stops it (the first complete answer's Slack timestamp, not a self-reported time). Give the speed bands and their points, keep speed scored separately from correctness, and say what a late, partial or missing answer gets and whether a later edit counts.
8. **Audit before posting.** Go through the paper question by question: does it break a constraint, overlap a taken question or topic, or rest on a guessed date or number? Fix or replace it. Then post the paper and the answer key together (the key in a reply or a file if the asker wants it hidden), unless they asked for the key later.
